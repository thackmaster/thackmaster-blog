It‘s not a secret that AI is unpopular. And it's not hard to see why. Unless of course you are 1), blinded by dollar signs, and/or 2) you get rock hard thinking about the damage you can cause the environment.

Now, it would be unfair of me to group every possible instance of AI under this umbrella as there are some instances where AI has been revolutionary and extremely helpful. For example, in the medical field. AI has shown that it is better at detecting cancer cells. So yes, there are some benefits to AI. Which brings me to my next point: we need to know what type of AI we’re talking about. The example of AI in the medical field is an example of ??? AI. The AI you might be familiar with is known as “Generative AI”, meaning it’s purpose is to generate content (text, images, video, code, etc.).

Is it odd that me, a technically inclined individual, am voicing my disdain for AI? Surprisingly, no. A lot of people in very similar positions as myself are doing the same thing. We're not fully against AI and I don't think the majority of the populous is. I believe that most simply want regulations to not exclude AI, but rather include it and be reformed to regulate it like anything else.

Why do you think it is that the chatbot, the "ask-me-anything" bot became as popular as it did? Because everyone could use it in one way or another, which turns into leverage to continue development and expansion of the field. But also because that's what is profitable. AI companies are losing money hand over fist, that's no secret. What is secret is just how much they're losing. Because once they give up the facade, there's no going back.

Sure, you might argue that we need to beat China in this sphere. To which I ask "why?" Aside from the cybersecurity ramifications of AI (which is not what the majority of AI is being developed to target) and the medical field, is there really a use for AI? Is it making companies more efficient? Is it making better choices than a human?
